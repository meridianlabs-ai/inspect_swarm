Analysis

Overview

When the swarm ends it writes two entries to the sample store, each also logged as an InfoEvent with source inspect_swarm:

  • inspect_swarm:metrics: tokens, cost and time for the swarm and each member, and counts of how each member acted, checked its work, communicated and waited (Metrics).
  • inspect_swarm:ledger: the swarm’s accounting. Its budget, caps, the usage of each member, model calls that errored or reported no usage, unpriced models, and what stopped the swarm (The ledger).

The result record is an event in the transcript, which swarm_result(sample) reads from a sample. It is described in Scoring.

Metrics

For the swarm:

  • tokens, cost: everything in the swarm: members, controller and final step.
  • wall_clock: seconds from the swarm’s start to its end.
  • never_acted: members with no completed model call.
  • steps_to_answer: completed model calls of the member whose output is the answer, null with no answer.
  • longest_member_steps: the largest number of completed model calls of any member.
  • critical_steps_to_answer: the number of completed model calls on the longest chain of calls the answer depended on, across members (The critical path).

Step counts include cache replays and exclude failed or cancelled attempts. A member’s subagents’ calls count as the member’s. steps_to_answer and longest_member_steps bound each member’s own sequential depth from above, not the swarm’s: they do not see that one member waited for or read another’s work.

For each member, in roster order:

  • tokens, cost: its usage, its subagents’ and background work’s included.
  • running_time: seconds from its start to its end, null for a member never started.
  • longest_identical_tool_run: the longest run of identical tool calls (same function and arguments), a sign of a stuck member. null for a bridged member, whose tool calls leave no tool events.
  • gate_checks, gate_passes, first_pass_time: its in-loop checks (Scoring).
  • comm, waits, waits_by_outcome, wait_time, idle_time: below.

The critical path

critical_steps_to_answer is the measure to read latency by: how many model calls the answer took one after another, given that members work in parallel but wait on and build on each other. A member that waits for a peer’s file and then answers in three steps has steps_to_answer 3, but if the peer took ten steps to write that file, the answer took at least 11.

Each member’s calls, its subagents’ and background work’s included, form one chain in the order they began. Chains link between members in two ways:

  • A view of a shared_memory file, or a swarm_wait result that names a file, follows the writes it showed. A view of a file follows every write in its history; a view of a deleted file follows the delete. Directory listings and the changes status line make no link.
  • A swarm_wait result that names a member’s end follows all of that member’s calls.

A wait that also reached a custom waiting channel links no files, since its evidence does not say which channel named them. Its links to member ends count either way.

The chain ends when the answering member ends, after its background work has finished, since that work can still change the member’s output.

The number over-counts work inside a member. Parallel and background subagents, tool calls of one turn, and any other concurrent work count one after another, and the answering member’s background work after its agent returned, its reads included, counts whether or not it changed the answer. That excess reaches peers through the member’s later writes.

The number also misses links. Only shared_memory and swarm_wait make them, so information that members share in other ways adds none: messages, a shared sandbox, a shared volume, a service, a custom channel and its wait results, a shared tool or store key, or a controller that passes one member’s result to another. A swarm_wait result that reports more than ten files names only the first ten in its evidence, so the rest make no link until the member views them. Missed links can only lower the number.

With both effects it is not a bound on true causal depth in either direction. A nested swarm’s board makes no link for the outer swarm; its own metrics count it.

It is null when there is no answer, when the answering member has no recorded end, when the board’s evidence is inconsistent (with a warning in the log), and for a sample resumed from a checkpoint. Metrics written by earlier versions do not have it.

Communication

Each member’s comm has an entry, by channel name, for each channel with records, such as shared_memory:

  • records and record_bytes: its writes or messages delivered to the channel, and their size.
  • rejections: its writes or messages the swarm refused, by the control that refused them, such as rate.
  • reads and listings: its views of files and of directories, and its read_messages results that showed messages.

A member’s subagents count as the member. Every member has an entry for every such channel the swarm opened, zeros included. A swarm with none (filesystem only, or channels=[]) has comm {} for every member: communication through files is observed, not recorded, so it is not reported as zero (Channels).

Waits

Each member’s metrics count its swarm_wait calls, its subagents’ included:

  • waits: the number of waits.
  • waits_by_outcome: that number by what ended each wait: news (a changed file or an ended member), all_waiting, stopping, member_ending, timeout or cancelled. An outcome that ended no wait is left out.
  • wait_time: the waits’ total seconds. Waits of a member’s subagents that overlap each count.
  • idle_time: the seconds the member did nothing but wait: its own agent in swarm_wait, not yet released, and none of its background work running. A subagent’s wait adds to wait_time but not to idle_time.

In a swarm with no swarm_wait (filesystem only, or channels=[]) the four are null, so 0 means the wait was offered and unused. Waiting is wall-clock time, so a member’s running_time includes it.

Wiring checks

The metrics also check that members are wired to the channels. tools_unwired lists, in a swarm with a channel that has tools, the members with a completed model call whose loops never resolved swarm_tools(). A member listed there did not get what the channel offers, so it ran a different experiment from the one configured.

Samples dataframe

inspect_swarm.analysis.SwarmColumns adds each sample’s realized cost and ledger data to Inspect’s samples_df(). samples_df() needs pandas and pyarrow, which Inspect Swarm does not install:

pip install "pandas>=2.1.0" "pyarrow>=10.0.1"

This example uses a task with a scorer named match. Replace score_match with your task’s score column (score_<scorer name>):

from inspect_ai.analysis import SampleSummary, samples_df
from inspect_ai.scorer import value_to_float

from inspect_swarm.analysis import SwarmColumns

df = samples_df("logs", columns=SampleSummary + SwarmColumns)
df = df[~df["swarm_resumed"]]
df["score"] = df["score_match"].map(value_to_float(), na_action="ignore")
df.groupby("log")[["score", "swarm_cost"]].mean()

The columns:

  • swarm_cost: the sample’s priced cost in dollars, from its model_usage, including scorer calls. Tokens are in SampleSummary’s total_tokens.
  • swarm_complete: no call is known to be missing from swarm_cost. The ledger marks the swarm’s interval complete, and every model in the sample’s model_usage is priced. Calls outside the swarm, including scorer calls, are not checked. False for a sample with no ledger.
  • swarm_members: members in the roster.
  • swarm_resumed: the sample resumed from a checkpoint, so the swarm restarted its caps from zero. Swarms now refuse checkpointing (Restrictions), so only logs from earlier versions can have it true. False for a sample with no ledger.
  • swarm_cost_cap, swarm_token_cap, swarm_time_cap: the swarm’s caps. The time cap is in seconds.
  • swarm_errored, swarm_no_usage, swarm_unpriced: model calls in the swarm that errored or were cancelled (retries included), that completed with no usage, and whose usage has no cost. Each makes the cost a lower bound.
  • swarm_unpriced_models: models with tokens and no cost, from the ledger and the sample’s model_usage, as a JSON list.

The columns read from the ledger are null for a sample with no ledger. A sample that runs two swarms keeps only the last one’s ledger, while its model_usage covers both. The columns read the sample’s store and model_usage, so do not exclude either with samples_df(exclude_fields=...).

The columns hold no per-member data. swarm_ledger() reads a sample’s whole ledger as a SwarmLedger, whose members are MemberLedgers with a Meter and Calls each. All are in inspect_swarm.analysis, and SwarmLedger.model_json_schema() describes every field. For example, each member’s cost:

from inspect_ai.log import read_eval_log

from inspect_swarm.analysis import swarm_ledger

log = read_eval_log("logs/my-eval.eval")
for sample in log.samples or []:
    ledger = swarm_ledger(sample)
    if ledger is not None:
        for m in ledger.members:
            print(sample.id, sample.epoch, m.name, m.meter.cost)

The metrics have no reader of their own; read them from sample.store["inspect_swarm:metrics"].

  • budget is the Budget as given. caps are the caps the swarm applied, derived from the sample’s limits or given: cost in dollars, tokens, and time in seconds, null for no cap. sample_limits are the sample’s limits when the swarm started.
  • members has one entry per member, in roster order. status is as in How a member ends, null for a member never started. cancelled means the swarm or the sample cancelled the member, so calls it had in flight are unknown. A hard stop during a background drain also sets cancelled; the agent’s returned status is kept. background_started counts the tasks the member started with background(), and background_cancelled those cancelled before they finished, whose calls in flight are unknown too.
  • meter is the usage and cost of what a member did. suspended_cost is the part spent while token counting was suspended, such as approval calls.
  • calls counts model calls: calls completed ones other than cache replays, cache_replays replayed from cache at no charge, errored attempts that errored or were cancelled (retries included), no_usage completed with no usage, and unpriced completed with usage but no cost.
  • runtime meters the controller’s own calls, final the final step’s (the default first-submission answer makes none), and swarm everything in the swarm: members, runtime and final step. The top-level calls counts every call in the swarm. unpriced_calls_recorded counts the unpriced calls the swarm’s meter recorded.
  • sample_delta is the sample’s usage while the swarm ran. unattributed is sample_delta minus swarm: usage no meter holds, such as the tokens of approval calls.
  • unpriced_models are models with tokens but no cost. native_compaction_classes are loaded model classes that can compact a conversation natively, which logs nothing, so their usage may be missing.
  • overshoot is how far usage went past each cap, for caps it passed. Calls in flight at a stop can carry usage past a cap.
  • stop_reason is why the swarm stopped itself: a controller’s submitted or all_done (as leaderless returns), verified or quiescence, controller:<reason> for any other reason a controller returns, swarm_cap or swarm_time. ended_by is what ended it from outside: sample_limit:<type>, operator for an interrupt, or unknown. Each is null when it did not happen. Both are set when a sample limit ends the swarm during its own stop.
  • interrupted means an error (a member’s, the controller’s or the final step’s) or a cancellation ended the swarm before it finished, final_cancelled that the final step was cancelled, and resumed that the sample resumed from a checkpoint.
  • total_complete means no call is known to be missing from the totals: nothing was cancelled or interrupted, every call reported priced usage, and no model class can compact natively. Otherwise the totals are lower bounds. attribution_complete adds that unattributed is zero.
